Your money, on your Mac.

Margin shows where your money comes from and where it goes, what your investments are doing, and your net worth. It reads your bank alerts and statements every night, and keeps all of it on your Mac, behind Touch ID.

What it does

Everything your banks tell you, in one place.

Reads what your banks send

Alerts in Gmail (read-only), the bank texts your iPhone forwards to your Mac or your Android phone backs up, and the statements you download. It syncs every night by itself.

Spending

Money in and out by month or pay cycle, by category and payee. Pick a category once and past and future payments follow.

Investments and net worth

Stocks, funds, retirement accounts and deposits, valued every night, with price alerts. Alerts only: Margin never trades.

Reviews and checks

Daily, weekly and monthly summaries, plus checks for double charges, unusual amounts and new cards.

India and the US

HDFC, ICICI, SBI, Axis, Kotak; Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Capital One, Amex and more. CDSL, NPS, EPF and PPF; Fidelity, Schwab and Vanguard downloads. Any home currency.

Any AI, or none

The AI you already use (Claude, Codex, Gemini, an API service, or a model on your Mac through Ollama) sorts what rules can’t. Every change it makes can be undone.

Private by design

It runs on your Mac, not on a server.

There’s no account to sign up for and no company holding your data. Margin is a program on your Mac that reads what your banks already send you.

On your Mac, nowhere else

Your data sits in a folder only your Mac user can read. The page is served by your Mac to your Mac, and locks with Touch ID when you step away.

No bank login

Margin never connects to your bank: no bank passwords, no Plaid, no aggregator. Gmail is read-only, through your own Google sign-in.

Open source

Every line is on GitHub under the MIT licence. A leak guard keeps personal details out of the code.

What leaves your Mac, and only if you use it

  • Your AI gets masked text to sort and review, with its tools off. With Ollama on your Mac, nothing leaves.
  • US prices: Yahoo Finance gets the tickers you hold. Indian prices come from whole-market files: nothing about you is sent.
  • Exchange rates: currency codes and dates, to the European Central Bank’s rates.
  • Updates: once a night Margin asks GitHub whether a new release is out. It can be turned off.

Install

Set it up in about half an hour.

You need a Mac and its command line tools. Most of the time goes into connecting Gmail, step by step. An AI is optional.

  1. Get the Mac’s command line tools

    Once. Click Install when it asks; this brings Python and Swift too.

    xcode-select --install

  2. Get Margin

    Into any folder you like.

    git clone https://github.com/<owner>/margin.git ~/projects/margin
cd ~/projects/margin

  3. Set it up

    With Claude Code, in that folder: run claude and paste this. It loads Margin’s setup skill and the guard that keeps it out of your data.

    Set up Margin on this Mac for me with the margin-setup skill. Do every step you can, tell me exactly what to do for the steps only I can do, and never look at my money data.

    Or by yourself

    ./margin-cli setup

    Either way, it shows what it will add to your Mac and asks first, then walks you through the steps only you can do: signing in to Google, bank texts, statement passwords.

  4. Stay up to date

    Margin comes in numbered releases. When one is out, Margin says so. margin update shows what’s in it, asks, installs exactly that release and runs the tests, going back by itself if they fail. It never touches your data.

Good to know

What it doesn’t do (yet).

  • macOS only. It’s built on the Mac’s Keychain, Touch ID and PDF reader.
  • Setup happens in Terminal, or with Claude Code or another coding agent doing it with you. There’s no installer app yet.
  • Email alerts come from Gmail. Connecting it means making your own Google Cloud project, about 10 minutes, step by step.
  • Android texts arrive up to a day late, through a daily backup app.
  • It knows what your banks tell you: their alerts and the statements you download. Banks outside India and the US need AI sorting.
  • Early days. It’s at version 0.x. It’s not financial advice, and it never trades.
